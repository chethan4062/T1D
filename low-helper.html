Low blood sugar

Is someone having a low?

Shaky, sweaty, pale, confused or suddenly very hungry. This will walk you through it, one step at a time.

First question: are they awake and able to swallow?

This is general first-aid information, not medical advice, and not a medical device. If they have their own care plan, follow that. If you are unsure at any point, call your local medical emergency number.

Step 1 of 3

Give fast sugar now

Any one of these. About 15 grams of sugar.

  • Juicehalf a cup (120 ml)
  • Regular sodahalf a cup, not diet
  • Glucose tablets3 to 4 tablets
  • Sugar or honey1 tablespoon

Not diet drinks, and not chocolate: it works too slowly. Small children may need less.

Step 2 of 3 · Wait

15:00until you check

Stay with them

Have them sit down. The sugar needs time to work.

Keep this screen open. It should chime when the time is up, but don't rely on the alarm alone: keep an eye on the clock and on them.

Step 3 of 3

Check their blood sugar

Ask them to check with their meter or sensor. No way to check? Go by how they feel.

In range means above 70 mg/dL (about 4 mmol/L).

Well done. Now a snack.

The fast sugar wears off quickly, so they need something that lasts.

  • Give a snack with carbs and proteinA sandwich, crackers with cheese or peanut butter, or a glass of milk. Skip this if a meal is coming soon.
  • Stay a little longerThey may feel tired, shaky or have a headache for a while.
  • No driving yetThey should wait until they feel fully back to normal.

Emergency

Call your local medical emergency number now

Use your phone to call. Say “diabetic, unconscious”.

  1. Nothing in their mouthNo food, no drink. They could choke.
  2. Lay them on their sideTop knee bent, head tilted back a little so they can breathe.
  3. Give glucagon, if you know howMany people with diabetes carry it as a nasal spray or injection. Check their bag.
  4. Stay until help arrivesIf they wake up and can swallow, give fast sugar.
